EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS - INTERPRETATION REPORT

Total Records: 9,475,848 | Meters: 1,000 | Data Completeness: 99.70%

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

The EDA reveals strong temporal patterns in Slovak household energy consumption,
with clear daily, weekly, and seasonal cycles. However, weather impact analysis
is severely limited by data availability issues from the Meteostat API, particularly
for precipitation (99.98% missing) and temperature (22.07% missing).

Key Insight: Energy consumption is primarily driven by human behavioral patterns
(time of day, weekday/weekend) and seasonal temperature variations, with a
moderate negative correlation to temperature (-0.0597).

================================================================================
1. TEMPORAL CONSUMPTION PATTERNS - INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

DAILY PATTERNS:
---------------
• Peak Hour: 21:00 (4.542 kWh) - Evening peak when families are home
• Valley Hour: 15:00 (3.676 kWh) - Afternoon minimum during work/school hours
• Peak-to-Valley Ratio: 1.24x - Relatively modest variation

Interpretation: The consumption pattern follows typical residential behavior:
- Morning rise (6-9 AM): 3.984 kWh - People preparing for work/school
- Daytime low (10 AM-4 PM): ~3.7-3.9 kWh - Many residents away from home
- Evening peak (5-9 PM): 4.282 kWh - Cooking, heating, entertainment
- Night decline (10 PM-5 AM): ~3.9-4.0 kWh - Sleep period

WEEKLY PATTERNS:
----------------
• Weekday Average: 4.262 kWh
• Weekend Average: 3.540 kWh (83% of weekday consumption)
• Peak Day: Wednesday (4.336 kWh)
• Lowest Day: Sunday (3.415 kWh)

Interpretation: 17% lower weekend consumption suggests:
- Commercial/industrial influence in residential areas
- Different lifestyle patterns (going out, traveling)
- Less structured daily routines on weekends

HOLIDAY EFFECT:
---------------
• Holiday consumption: 3.409 kWh (16.42% lower than non-holidays)

Interpretation: Similar to weekends, indicating reduced economic activity
and potentially travel away from primary residences.

----------------------------

----------------------------------------

2. 99 ZIP codes (30.3%) without any weather coverage
3. Rural areas particularly affected
4. Precipitation data essentially unusable for analysis

IMPACT ON ANALYSIS:

• Temperature analysis limited to 78% of meters
• Regional weather comparisons incomplete
• Weather-based prediction models will have reduced accuracy

================================================================================
CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

The Slovak energy consumption dataset reveals clear, predictable patterns driven
primarily by human behavior and seasonal variations rather than weather conditions.
The lack of correlation with temperature indicates that **electricity is not the primary heating source** for these households. Consequently, weather-based features will have limited predictive power, not just due to data limitations, but due to the fundamental nature of the consumption.

The strong temporal patterns (hourly, daily, weekly, seasonal) provide a solid
foundation for time-series forecasting. The 17% weekend effect and 16% holiday
reduction are substantial and consistent, making them valuable predictive features.

The extreme variation in household consumption (0 to 763 kWh/day) and right-skewed
distribution suggest the presence of non-residential meters or data quality issues
that need addressing before modeling.

KEY RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Focus modeling efforts on temporal patterns rather than weather
2. Implement nearest-neighbor imputation for missing weather data
3. Consider separate models for consumption tiers
4. Investigate and potentially remove extreme outliers
5. Acknowledge precipitation data as unusable for this analysis

The Meteostat API limitations significantly constrain weather-based analysis,
but the rich temporal patterns provide sufficient information for accurate
demand forecasting and customer insights.
